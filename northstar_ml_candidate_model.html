ML Candidate Model

Discovery rules are precise but only find what they were written for. This small, explainable model reads field names and descriptions, learns from steward-labelled examples, and adds the fields the rules miss to the steward’s review list — with the words behind every score. It never decides a verdict, a policy result or a reuse request.

Business objective

Miss fewer critical data elements at design time without adding noise for stewards.

On 12 hand-written fields never used in training. Illustrative, not a performance claim.

The problem

Brittle rules

Rules find only what they name

A rule for “balance” and “due date” misses a credit limit or an APR, even though both drive credit outcomes.

Steward knowledge is lost

Every decision is made once

Stewards decide the same kinds of fields across applications, but nothing learns from those decisions.

Black boxes are not acceptable

A score must explain itself

In a bank, a model that flags data must show why, be reproducible and be validated before production.

Guardrails, by design

Adds, never removes

It can put a field on the review list; it can never take one off.

Never decides

Verdicts, policy results and reuse decisions stay rule-based and human-approved.

Explains every score

Score = bias + word weights. The top reasons travel with the suggestion.

Metadata only

Reads names and descriptions — never data values or customer data.

Why not a large language model here? Discovery runs on every field of every spec and must be reproducible and cheap to validate. A linear model with word weights is both. An approved LLM can still explain a suggestion in plain language — it never produces the score.
Training data

What the model learns from

123 hand-written, synthetic field records from a card-billing estate, each labelled the way a data steward would: cde = worth reviewing as a possible CDE, not_cde = not. The label is a review recommendation, never an official designation.

How the data is split

Training · 99 rows

Rows are interleaved (CDE, not, CDE, not…) and four of every five go to training. The model learns its weights only from these.

Held out · 24 rows

Every fifth interleaved row. Never trained on; scored on the Train tab. Similar wording to training, so its scores flatter the model.

Separate test · 12 fields

Written independently and never in training (a self-check enforces it). Used for rules vs rules + model. The notebook adds a stricter split by whole topic.

Deliberate gaps and traps. Hardship-programme fields are left out so the Learn tab can show one steward decision teaching a new topic. Three “no effect on … credit” display fields were added so negated wording does not look like a decision field. Steward feedback, when recorded, is added with weight ×3.

Model card

Intended useSuggest additional CDE candidates in project artifacts for steward review. Decision support only.
Out of scopeDesignation, policy compliance, verdicts, reuse or access decisions, anything about customers.
Training dataSynthetic card-billing fields with steward-style labels, plus steward feedback.
Known limitsBag of words: weak on negation and abbreviations, English only; synthetic data inflates scores; unknown words fall back to the bias.
Before productionReal labels across applications, out-of-application test set, drift monitoring, independent validation under the bank’s model-risk policy (for example, US Fed SR 11-7).
