Every critical-data decision should come with its evidence, its owner and its next action.

A project team designing a billing API does not know which fields are critical, which already carry approved application tags, or what evidence is required before release. Northstar compares the proposed design against the application CDE inventory and policy references, then prepares an evidence-backed decision for the steward.

The problem we solve

Late discovery

Criticality is found after design

CDE definitions live in catalogs and policy documents. Reviews happen late, so important fields can ship without an owner, lineage or tested controls.

Tags ≠ readiness

An approved tag says nothing about today

A field approved last year can have missing lineage, a failed quality test and stale evidence. Teams need both answers, separately.

Institutional knowledge

Schemas alone cannot answer it

A schema-only classifier cannot tell what is already tagged or what a policy demands. Access to the inventory and policy references is the differentiator.

The flagship's four decisions

Keep them separate — each has a different owner.

01 · PRESENCE

Is there critical data?

Does the submitted scope contain approved CDEs or potential new candidates?

e.g. paymentDueDate → untagged candidate
02 · MAPPING

Does the tag really match?

Does the inventory record refer to the same field, meaning, type, version and use?

e.g. wrong application ID → review, never inherit
03 · READINESS

Is the evidence current?

Are ownership, source, lineage, quality and approval evidence present and in date?

e.g. statementBalance → lineage UNKNOWN, test FAIL
04 · ACTION

Who fixes what?

Who resolves each gap, and what goes into the proposed data contract?

e.g. steward nominates; control owner attaches rerun
Three rules the demo makes visible. An officially approved CDE can have failed controls. A new candidate can have excellent controls without being officially classified. A sensitive field is not automatically a CDE.

Why this flagship

What makes it useful

Reconcile design evidence against approved tags before assigning new ones. Detect missing candidates, alias-only matches, stale approvals, conflicting tags, fields absent from scope, missing controls and missing source evidence. Keep the discovery verdict separate from control readiness.

MVP + architecture

One application; JSON/OpenAPI with local references; limited YAML/SQL/text fallback; one inventory export and a reviewed structured policy pack. Parse → match → deterministic checks → evidence packet → steward. An approved LLM can later explain semantic matches with retrieval citations.

01b · Flagship add-on · machine learning

CDE Candidate Model

A small model trained on steward-labelled fields learns which words signal criticality. It catches what the rules miss, explains every score, and improves each time a steward records a decision. It may only add fields to the review list — it never decides.

What it learns

Which words in names and descriptions signal criticality — “credit line”, “rate”, “delinquency” — and which signal the opposite — “display-only”, “session”, “no effect on”.

What it may do

Put a field on the steward's review list, with a score and the words behind it. Nothing else.

What it may never do

Change the YES / POTENTIAL / NO verdict, a policy result or a catalog tag. Rules and the steward stay in charge.

02 · Flagship release proof

CDE Guardian

Will this change break an important consumer? Guardian diffs two contract versions, traces the declared lineage, and gates the release on versioned rules plus explicit evidence. A generated migration plan cannot unblock the gate by itself.

Worked example

An engineer changes statementBalance from decimal dollars to integer cents and renames customerEmail. Autopay still reads dollars. The gate stops the release until a compatible version, consumer tests and owner approval exist.

Refinement

Separate known breakage from incomplete lineage or uncertain model findings. The LLM writes the explanation; deterministic assertions decide. Exceptions need an owner and an expiry.

Avoid

Estimating customer blast radius from incomplete lineage. This view names affected consumers, never invented customer counts.

How it differs from the simulator

The simulator injects failures to test resilience. Sentinel compares the two actual representations in the supplied fixtures. For the three balances, the units error produces an absolute discrepancy of $180,788.85 — a dataset discrepancy, not measured customer loss.

Configure a defensible workflow

1 · Establish scope and authorityApplication ID, business process, jurisdiction, effective date, domain steward and inventory completeness. Preserve original catalog IDs and approval status.
2 · Normalize policy referencesPer clause: policy ID, version, section, effective date, requirement, applicability, expected evidence, severity, exception authority. A policy owner validates each extracted rule.
3 · Separate retrieval from decisionsThe enterprise LLM retrieves applicable text and explains candidate mappings with exact citations. Deterministic validators check structured evidence; unresolved interpretation stays UNKNOWN.
4 · Compare existing application tagsExact field/path, approved ID and approval date first. Alias or semantic matches need steward confirmation. Missing scope is not a reason to delete a tag.
5 · Keep strategy advisoryDomain standards, source reuse, duplication and ownership. An aligned strategy choice never overrides a failed mandatory control.
6 · Close through the accountable workflowOwner actions, due dates, review rationale, evidence versions and exception expiry. Integration writes need authorization; this demo exports proposals only.
